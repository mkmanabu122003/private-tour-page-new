Quick Decision

For most visitors, start with Tsukiji.

It has better food variety, more atmosphere, and no reservation needed. Toyosu is worth it when the tuna auction is the main reason for your morning.

Tsukiji
Food stalls, shops, old Tokyo texture
Toyosu
Auction viewing, modern market facility

"Should I go to Tsukiji or Toyosu?" This is one of the most common questions I get from visitors planning their Tokyo itinerary. The internet is full of contradictory advice because one part of Tsukiji moved and another part stayed.

The new structure makes the article feel more guided: a clear recommendation, then the nuance. Long paragraphs are broken by decision modules that answer the traveler's next question before fatigue sets in.

Choose Tsukiji if...

  • You want breakfast, snacks, and street-level atmosphere.
  • You have limited time and want fewer logistics.
  • You enjoy shops, knives, tea, and ceramics.

Choose Toyosu if...

  • You care about the tuna auction more than browsing.
  • You prefer clean, spacious facilities.
  • You already secured an auction viewing slot.

What Actually Moved in 2018?

The wholesale market moved to Toyosu. The outer market, where most visitors eat and shop, stayed in Tsukiji. This section should use one tight diagram-like sentence block, not a dense history lesson.

Guide Note

I take guests to both markets. My default plan is Tsukiji first, then Toyosu only when the auction is part of the morning.
